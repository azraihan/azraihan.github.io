  1. Under Review
    Learning What to Break: Adaptive Soft Equivariance for Imperfect Symmetry Beyond Natural Images
    Ruwad Naswan*, Abrar Zahin Raihan*, and Md Ashiqur Rahman
    Abstract

    Equivariance to rotations is a desirable property for vision models applied beyond natural images, where objects appear at arbitrary orientations. However, real-world visual data only approximately satisfies symmetry assumptions: directional illumination, tissue polarity, and asymmetric anatomy break symmetry locally. Strictly equivariant networks cannot break symmetry at all, while existing soft-equivariant methods break it in a fixed way that ignores the task. To effectively handle such imperfect symmetry, we present Adaptive Soft Equivariance, a framework that learns what symmetries to break. We partition each layer's weight space into an invariant subspace and a symmetry-breaking subspace, and train a binary selector to allocate a softness budget along directions that minimize the task loss. Adaptive Soft Equivariance can be easily incorporated into existing convolutional and transformer architectures and adapted to pre-trained foundation models by filtering only their input interface. Notably, we show that across remote sensing, digital pathology, and both 2D and 3D medical image segmentation, our method improves both accuracy and rotation consistency for popular deep nets, e.g., ViT, ConvNeXt, nnU-Net, and MedNeXt.

  2. Under Review
    Not All in One View: Bringing Evidence Together for Training-Free Chart and Table Question Answering
    Abrar Zahin Raihan*, Aurchi Chowdhury*, and Nazia Tasnim
    Abstract

    Multimodal large language models struggle to read fine-grained evidence in charts and tables under constrained visual-token budgets. Training-free cropping can improve evidence access, but a single window must balance coverage of separated evidence against the detail needed to read it. We propose Component-wise Rank-1 Refinement (CR1R), which separates visually supported grounding responses into connected components and selects complementary crops without updating model parameters. CR1R adjusts the importance scores within each region to favor consistent row and column patterns, while leaving locations with zero scores unchanged. Experiments across ten chart and table benchmarks and four frozen backbones show downstream gains, with up to a 4.86-percentage-point improvement in table macro accuracy over the entropy-gradient grounding baseline, alongside better evidence alignment in all ten evaluated backbone-map pairings. Further results show that these views can complement extracted text and higher global resolution, with benefits extending beyond chart and table QA. Together, these findings establish a practical way to improve access to chart and table evidence and highlight evidence organization as a useful direction for training-free multimodal inference.

  3. Under Review
    MemPMI: Test-Time Auditing of World-Model Memory
    Abrar Zahin Raihan*, Aurchi Chowdhury*, and Nazia Tasnim
    Abstract

    Video world models keep a memory of past frames to stay consistent when the camera returns to a place. When the world changes between visits, a memory frame can show a state that no longer holds, and a model that trusts it draws the old state. Recency, camera overlap and image similarity all rate such out-of-date memory as a good match. We propose MemPMI, a training-free, test-time audit that asks the frozen world model itself. MemPMI scores a memory by the model's denoising loss with irrelevant memories minus its loss with the memory. With the weighting of the evidence lower bound (ELBO), this score estimates the pointwise mutual information (PMI) between the view and the memory. It has the same zero point for every model, and its map over the view shows where a memory contradicts it. On eight released world models and five datasets, MemPMI detects out-of-date memory, tells which of two views a memory contradicts, and locates the change better than image, camera, learned and raw-likelihood baselines, even when the memory was seen from a very different angle. Without generating video, it also measures how much each model uses its memory, and forgetting the frames it flags improves later predictions.

  1. NeurIPS
    MedCAR: Conflict-Aware Multi-Agent Reasoning for Multimodal Clinical AI
    Mst. Fahmida Sultana Naznin, Niloy Kumar Mondal, Mohammad Ninad Mahmud Nobo, Abrar Zahin Raihan, and Sheikh Azizul Hakim
    In Proceedings of the First Workshop on World Models for High-Stakes Health (WMHS) at NeurIPS 2026 (Atlanta),
    and
    In Proceedings of the Third Workshop on GenAI for Health: Agentic Systems, Clinical Trust, and Future Potential at NeurIPS 2026 (Sydney)    , Dec 2026
    Accepted · Non-archival · Poster
    Paper will be available after the proceedings.
    Abstract OpenReview Code

    Multimodal generative AI transforms healthcare by integrating radiology images, pathology data, EHRs, and clinical reports for unified decision support. Modern systems use LLM-driven multi-agent frameworks with tools such as diagnostic predictors, segmentation models, VQA modules, and report generators for complex clinical reasoning. However, cross-modal conflicts (inconsistent diagnoses, implausible segmentations, and contradictory reports) reduce reliability and trust. We propose MedCAR (Medical Conflict-Aware Reasoner), a conflict-aware multi-agent framework with: (1) probabilistic-semantic conflict detection via neural entailment, (2) Graph-Based Anatomical Consistency Learning (GACL), and (3) bipolar weighted argumentation with adaptive trust calibration and abstention. We also introduce ChestAgentBench-X, a benchmark of 503 multimodal chest X-ray cases with tool-level conflicts. MedCAR achieves 58.57% overall accuracy on ChestAgentBench-X, outperforming the strongest baseline (54.47%), and 71.8% overall accuracy on the larger 2,500-question ChestAgentBench, outperforming GPT-4o and MedRAX. These results highlight conflict-aware reasoning as key for reliable multimodal clinical AI.

  2. ACL
    Causal Localization of the English Pivot in LLaVA: Mechanistic VLM Analysis and Training-Free Multilingual Steering
    Abrar Zahin Raihan* and Aurchi Chowdhury*
    In Proceedings of the 1st Workshop on Multilinguality in the Era of Large Language Models (MeLLM) at ACL 2026, Jul 2026
    Accepted · Archival
    Abstract Paper Code

    Multilingual vision-language models (VLMs) consistently underperform on non-English visual queries, yet the internal mechanism behind this disparity remains unknown. As a focused case study on LLaVA-1.5-7B, we apply logit-lens analysis and causal activation patching to show that non-English visual queries are routed through an English-biased representational bottleneck in layers 5–17, extending the English-pivot phenomenon of Wendler et al. (2024) to the multimodal setting. Peak causal influence occurs at layer 8 (AIE=0.49, averaged across languages), with all measurable pivot signal running through text-token positions. Without meaningful visual content (blank-image condition), language-specific representations do not emerge at any layer, showing that the pivot is image-content-dependent rather than triggered by any visual input. Building on these findings, we derive training-free language-steering vectors at the mechanistically identified pivot layers, improving Russian VQA by +6.5 pp and Portuguese by +4.0 pp on MMMB without any fine-tuning — the latter surpassing the English baseline.

  3. CVPR
    Where Do Affordances Crystallize? Video vs. Image Self-Supervised Encoders for Embodied Perception
    Abrar Zahin Raihan* and Aurchi Chowdhury*
    In Proceedings of the 7th Annual Embodied AI (EAI) Workshop at CVPR 2026, Jun 2026
    Accepted · Non-archival · Poster
    Abstract Paper Code

    Self-supervised video models are strong backbones for robotic perception. We probe 25 layer positions of architecturally matched ViT-L encoders (V-JEPA 2, V-JEPA 2.1, DINOv2) on the UMD Part Affordance Dataset and show that video SSL models crystallize affordance signal at layer 19 — four layers earlier than DINOv2 (image SSL, layer 23), suggesting a consistent effect of temporal predictive objectives on representation depth. Both V-JEPA variants share the same peak depth, yet V-JEPA 2.1’s intermediate-layer supervision yields markedly stronger early-layer representations (layer 2 mAP: 92.2 vs. 85.0 for V-JEPA 2). Head ablation at each peak layer further reveals a sparse set of attention heads that concentrate the affordance signal; keeping only the top-3 heads achieves a 5.3× attention compute reduction with ≤0.1 mAP loss.
